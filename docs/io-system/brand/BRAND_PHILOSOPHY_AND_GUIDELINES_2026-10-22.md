# Indus Orbit — core philosophy and brand guidelines

**Review date:** 22 October 2026, Europe/Paris. **Implementation state: Planned** for the proposed visual evolution and page rollout. The observations below describe the current repository; existing approved identity remains in force. This is the strategy and production specification for a guideline book, not evidence that every page or asset already follows it.

## 1. The direction in one page

**Indus Orbit brings people and useful intelligence together through two main offerings: Messaging and Terminal.**

**Messaging** supports relationships and conversations between people and groups. **Terminal** supports useful work with intelligence, with understandable tools, permissions and evidence. Events, communities, learning, programmes and research are extensions or contexts around those two offerings. They help people find a reason to connect or something worthwhile to do. This is the latest owner-defined architecture and supersedes an events-first parent-brand hierarchy.

| Layer                              | Decision                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Name                               | **Indus Orbit**                                                                                                                                |
| Company definition, approved       | **The General Intelligence Company of India**                                                                                                  |
| Short line, approved               | **Intelligence, built together.**                                                                                                              |
| Main offerings, owner-defined      | **Messaging + Terminal**. Other experiences extend or give context to these two.                                                               |
| Core belief, proposed articulation | People become more capable when they can find one another, exchange knowledge and build with useful intelligence.                              |
| Practical promise                  | Help someone find a worthwhile next step, a person to share it with and the tools to carry it forward.                                         |
| Brand feeling                      | Welcoming, thoughtful, quietly ambitious and full of possibility.                                                                              |
| Visual world                       | People, Indian places, everyday work, learning, nature and shared possibility, expressed through considered pixel illustration and warm light. |
| Proposed brighter expression       | Lighter paper, open skies, warm peach and saffron light, visible human detail and a little atmospheric softness.                               |

The owner clarified that **the art style is the reference; planets, extraterrestrial imagery and space astronomy are outside the core brand direction**. “Orbit” expresses relationships and shared purpose. It does not require a cosmic visual world. The existing assets remain preserved; this document recommends where each belongs.

The owner further defined the confirmed framed-sky image as a **portal of opportunity**: a way to explain how a General Intelligence Company can help different people become more capable and contribute to a better society. This is the primary metaphor. Personal reflection can be one supporting meaning; the system must show shared opportunity across many people, places and forms of participation.

### The experience to aim for

“I can connect with people here, or work with intelligence. I understand which door to choose and what it can do for me.”

The dreamy quality should open a sense of possibility. The interface should make participation and useful work easy. A person should see **Messaging** and **Terminal** before having to decode the extensions, internal architecture or programme names.

## 2. What is established, and what this review proposes

### Observed and established

- The approved company definition and short line appear in the current brand route and design philosophy record.
- `/about` names the pillars **Connection · Synergy · Society**. Its text emphasizes relationships across cities and generations, shared effort and benefit beyond the centre.
- The current code uses **Fraunces + Inter**, warm parchment, deep indigo, saffron and monsoon green.
- The original pixel `io` mark is used across public, member and I/O surfaces.
- The illustration family includes dawn and dusk, banyans, rooftops, markets, hills, lanterns, people working together and a traveller facing a tall frame of sky.
- The owner's latest decision establishes **Messaging and Terminal as the two main offerings**. Events, communities, learning, programmes and research are extensions/context. The existing implementation and route structure must be distinguished from this newly clarified hierarchy; a source feature is not automatically released because it appears in the brand architecture.

### Proposed in this review

- Give those existing ideas one clear philosophy and a smaller, reusable visual grammar.
- Curate illustrations by meaning and craft. Pixelation alone does not make an image part of the identity.
- Use the confirmed framed-sky/traveller artwork as the reference for a portal of opportunity, shared possibility and the brighter light treatment.
- Reduce darkness and heavy overlays on editorial pages; use solid, readable surfaces for working information.
- Separate the core identity, editorial illustrations, real community evidence and merchandise concepts.
- Give the public narrative **two clear doors: Messaging and Terminal**. Use events, communities and other extensions to make their usefulness tangible. “Meet,” “learn” and “build” can remain ordinary verbs in context; they are not a new three-pillar product architecture or replacement tagline.

The proposed wording, palette extensions and curation decisions require design review before a broad rollout. They do not replace prior product permissions, verified service boundaries or release gates.

## 3. Purpose, audience and promise

### Purpose

Make the benefits of intelligence and collective effort more useful and more accessible to people in India and the wider Indian community.

### Who the experience serves

| Person                 | Immediate need                                                  | Brand responsibility                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First visitor          | Understand Messaging, Terminal and which is useful now          | Explain the two main offerings with accurate availability and two clear paths.          |
| Person using Messaging | Connect privately or with a relevant group                      | Make relationships, access, conversation state and recovery understandable.             |
| Person using Terminal  | Work with useful intelligence and tools                         | Explain setup, current capabilities, permissions, cost and evidence.                    |
| Event guest            | Know what, when, where, who and what happens after registration | Make the event page self-contained and honest about access, confirmation and waitlists. |
| Community member       | Find people, belonging, activity and useful continuity          | Preserve context and respect attention.                                                 |
| Host or contributor    | Bring people together and do worthwhile work                    | Make responsibilities, review and support clear.                                        |
| Builder using I/O      | Understand the intelligence/runtime context around Terminal     | Explain the term and show evidence and accurate operational states.                     |

### Promise and limits

“Intelligence, built together.” is a commitment to how the company works. It is not a claim that general artificial intelligence has been achieved, every provider is active, every event is reviewed to the same depth, or every visitor has access to every community. Capability and availability claims must match current evidence.

## 4. Values expressed as behaviour

Keep the existing three pillars. Express their meaning through concrete behaviour rather than repeating abstract nouns across every page.

| Established pillar | Human meaning                              | Product and communication proof                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Connection**     | Help people find one another with purpose. | Clear invitations, relevant communities, intelligible profiles, consent before contact and contextual conversations.                                                             |
| **Synergy**        | Help people make more together.            | Shared work, useful introductions, attribution, event follow-through and tools that reduce friction. In explanatory copy, prefer “shared work” or “build together” when clearer. |
| **Society**        | Let benefit reach beyond the centre.       | Accessible layouts, mobile and bandwidth consideration, multiple places and backgrounds, clear policies and honest limits.                                                       |

Five practical tests make these pillars reviewable:

1. **Agency:** Does the person understand the choice and retain control?
2. **Belonging:** Can a newcomer see a route to participation without performing status?
3. **Usefulness:** Does this screen or message help someone act or understand?
4. **Care:** Are privacy, moderation, accessibility and continuity visible in the experience?
5. **Truth:** Is a promise supported by something real, current and attributable?

These are proposed decision tests, not five additional public pillars or five new navigation items.

Use one parent identity with **Messaging and Terminal** as the two primary offerings. Use plain descriptors for everything that extends them. This architecture is the owner's latest decision; the navigation and wider design rollout remain proposed implementation work.

**Proposed public navigation:** Messaging · Terminal · Explore · About, with the account/sign-in action separate. Explore holds extensions such as events, communities, learning and programmes. This is a reviewable information-architecture proposal, not a claim that current routes have been moved or that every extension is ready.

### Capability truth: present versus aspiration

- The current Messaging candidate includes private messages and event/community group conversation capabilities in source. Publish its availability according to the release record; do not describe all candidate code as deployed production.
- The current Terminal/OpenCode path requires a compatible, locally running OpenCode server and explicit connection/setup. Explain this requirement where someone chooses Terminal. A hosted browser page must not imply that an arbitrary person's local computer is already connected.
- Actual provider execution depends on the configured runtime, permissions, availability and activation evidence. Brand copy must not turn a model name or provider record into an active-service claim.
- A seamless conversation-to-terminal workflow, shared terminal collaboration, automatic event follow-up and cross-product automation are **aspirational unless a specific current implementation and evidence demonstrate them**. Show them only as labelled concepts until verified and released.

Messaging and Terminal can have different information density while retaining the same type, colour roles, spacing discipline and voice. The metaphor of two doors explains a choice; it does not imply that a third party or the system makes that choice for the person. Technical administration stays operationally quiet.

## 6. Personality and voice

### Personality

- **Warm:** an invitation from a thoughtful person.
- **Lucid:** plain words, clear order and an understandable next step.
- **Rooted:** specific places, people and details, with room for contemporary India in all its variety.
- **Curious:** generous toward learning and new possibility.
- **Exact:** clear about time, place, state, authority, cost and uncertainty.

Warmth comes through welcome, detail and care. Premium quality comes through restraint, dependable behaviour and editing.

### Writing rules

1. Lead with what a person can do or why it matters.
2. Put the important noun and verb early.
3. Use one idea per short paragraph and one primary action per view.
4. Use “you” for the person's choice and “we” for an actual company responsibility.
5. Use human language for errors, waitlists and review. Explain what happens next.
6. Name a state accurately: upcoming, registration open, waitlisted, confirmed, preview or unavailable.
7. Reserve the company definition for identity and context. Do not make it every page's headline.
8. Keep the approved short line exact, including punctuation. New campaign copy must not silently become another tagline.

| Situation                 | Example to use                                                                      | Reason                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Homepage introduction     | “Messaging for people. A terminal for working with intelligence.”                   | Makes the two main offerings understandable.               |
| Messaging entry           | “Find your conversations and groups.”                                               | A functional entry, subject to actual access.              |
| Terminal setup            | “Connect a compatible local OpenCode server to use this Terminal.”                  | States a current setup requirement at the point of choice. |
| Empty event listing       | “There are no upcoming events here yet. Explore other communities.”                 | Honest and actionable.                                     |
| Waitlist                  | “You're on the waitlist. We'll email you if a place opens.”                         | States the actual state and next step.                     |
| Reviewed host application | “Your host application is being reviewed. We'll email you when there is an update.” | Avoids invented deadlines.                                 |
| Message retry             | “This message hasn't sent. Your draft is still here.”                               | Reassures with an observable fact.                         |
| I/O preview               | “This route is in preview. It isn't available for requests yet.”                    | Separates possibility from an active service.              |

Avoid generic superlatives, “revolutionary” claims, pressure to join, constant exclamation marks, mystical language in operational flows and unsupported scale statistics. Keep “Bharat ka dimaag.” out of current official short-line usage; existing artwork carrying it is an older concept pending review.

## 7. The illustration world

### Core subjects

People meeting, learning, making, preparing, talking and travelling toward meaningful opportunities. Contemporary and everyday Indian places: homes, rooftops, studios, classrooms, libraries, workshops, cafés, markets, streets, villages, hills and gardens. Cities and small towns should both feel like places where the future can be made.

The banyan suggests a place to gather and continuity across people. Dawn suggests a beginning. The lotus can suggest growth. A path suggests progress with agency. These are flexible visual meanings; they are not mandatory decorations for every page.

### Composition

- Give a scene one human or narrative focal point.
- Let environment support a story without competing with the headline or action.
- Prefer a few understandable relationships over a dense collage of symbols.
- Keep foreground silhouettes clear, midground human details visible and distance softer and lighter.
- Reserve a calm area for adjacent text. Essential information belongs in live text, outside the illustration where possible.
- Show mutual participation rather than one heroic founder above an anonymous crowd.

### What is outside the core direction

Planets, giant celestial bodies, alien worlds and cosmic portals; generic neon networks; glowing code as a substitute for usefulness; a constant museum of domes and monuments; magical computer imagery unrelated to actual product behaviour; decorative “ancient wisdom meets AI” tropes. A subject-specific article may require a scientific illustration, but its subject does not become the parent identity.

## 8. The confirmed image: a portal of opportunity

**Found and visually inspected:** `public/wiki-hero.jpg`, currently referenced by `src/routes/what-is-indus-orbit.tsx`.

The square illustration shows a traveller from behind, wearing saffron and carrying a bag, facing a tall freestanding frame containing a warm sky. The surrounding scene is a mountain valley with terraces, homes, paths and other people. There is no literal reflected face visible. The owner confirmed this is the intended reference.

### Primary meaning, clarified by the owner

**Opportunity opens when people can connect, learn and make useful things with intelligence.** The frame makes that idea visible: a familiar place opens onto a worthwhile next step and a wider shared possibility. The traveller chooses whether and how to participate. The other paths and people matter because the purpose extends to society.

The portal is a brand metaphor, not an extraterrestrial destination, supernatural promise or literal product feature. Through **Messaging**, opportunity may begin with a person or group; through **Terminal**, it may begin with a useful task or tool. Extensions provide the setting or purpose. The metaphor must not suggest that the application guarantees a job, funding, admission, transformation or an unlocked entitlement. Real actions and their conditions remain explicit.

Use this image for **What is Indus Orbit, an invitation to opportunity, orientation, a member's beginning or a thoughtful editorial story**. The same idea can take many grounded forms: a doorway into a workshop, a classroom window, an opening between trees, a studio threshold or a path toward people gathering. For events discovery, pair the metaphor with real people/event facts and a clear next action. It should not become the backdrop to every transactional screen.

### A repeatable system across people, places and formats

Build a coherent illustrated cast and scene grammar through future reviewed art briefs. Avoid making one lone traveller the permanent representative of everyone. Scenes can include a student learning with peers, a small-business owner improving a task, a maker sharing work, a mentor listening, neighbours organising a gathering or a team preparing something useful. Their connection to the portal is **an understandable opportunity to act together**.

Use urban and rural places, ordinary accessible settings and a range of ages, roles, backgrounds and disabilities across the collection. Include contemporary working life as well as heritage. Specificity and participation should carry inclusion; avoid costumes, a token person in every scene or symbolic diversity without a role in the story. Review accessibility details in the depicted space rather than adding them as decoration.

Across website heroes, event covers, Instagram cards, short video sequences, orientation and editorial pages, preserve the same relationships: a grounded place, a person with agency, an opening of possibility, other people or useful work, and warm light. Change the format and viewpoint while retaining the purpose and craft. This is a future art direction brief; a recurring cast and new variants are not yet produced or approved.

### Character rules

- Keep proportions, clothing, skin, hair, bags and facial detail coherent within a scene.
- Show varied ages, bodies, genders, abilities and places across the system without turning one image into a checklist.
- Let people work, choose and contribute. AI is a tool or quiet aid when relevant.
- Avoid spiritual authority, destiny, mind-reading or guaranteed transformation claims.
- Distinguish illustrated characters from real members, speakers and hosts. Never attach invented testimony or credentials to a generated character.
- Keep any future recurring character sheet with views, scale, palette, clothing and approved poses. Do not assume every person currently depicted is the same character.

## 9. Brighter and a little dreamy: art treatment

The confirmed image already contains the useful direction: peach light, blue distance, warm human colour and legible silhouettes. Evolve from that relationship.

### Treatment recipe for future reviewed variants

1. **Preserve the original master.** Make a separately named derivative with the source and review date recorded.
2. **Open the middle values.** Reveal people, faces, work surfaces and paths before brightening the whole sky. Keep a few deep indigo anchors.
3. **Move toward morning or warm late-day light.** Use pale peach, cream and quiet sky blue; keep saffron light local and purposeful.
4. **Add atmosphere through depth.** Distant forms can become lighter and lower contrast. Keep the foreground's important contours crisp.
5. **Use small pools of warmth.** A window, lantern or sunlit edge is enough. Continuous electric glow makes the scene louder.
6. **Simplify saturation.** Let the focal person or action hold the stronger colour. Leave generous quieter areas.
7. **Keep the pixel craft.** Work with deliberate clusters and a consistent apparent pixel scale. Avoid blur over character edges, text or the logo.
8. **Review at actual sizes.** A detailed 1024px illustration can become noise at 96px. Select another crop or a simpler asset rather than sharpening everything.

### Interface application

Lighten the page through parchment space and less image darkening. Place dark text on a solid light panel when an image would otherwise need a heavy overlay. Use a mild local scrim only when text really sits on art; verify the worst part of the crop. Keep forms, chat, event facts, availability and receipts sharp and still.

There is no approved global CSS “dream filter.” Applying brightness, saturation and blur to the entire application would also alter text, user photos, logos and status colours. Atmosphere belongs in selected artwork and restrained editorial backgrounds.

**Proposed review heuristics:** roughly two thirds quiet light surface on public content pages, dark areas as anchors, saffron as a small accent. These are composition guides, not rigid quotas and not current measured implementation facts.

Bright is an amount of light and space. It does not require low-contrast text or more saturated colours. Errors, warnings, success and information need separate semantic tokens, labels and icons; saffron should not carry every meaning.

## 11. Typography and information hierarchy

- **Fraunces:** selected editorial titles, meaningful quotes and short moments of welcome. Use regular or medium where fine strokes would be weak on a small screen or image.
- **Inter:** body copy, navigation, dates, event facts, buttons, forms, chat and operational information.
- **Monospace:** code and technical identifiers where useful, not a general retro aesthetic.

| Role                 | Proposed range                  | Rule                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main editorial title | 40–72px desktop; 32–44px mobile | Bound the line length and avoid one-word fragments.                       |
| Section title        | 28–40px desktop; 24–32px mobile | Give sections different meaning, not identical oversized headings.        |
| Reading body         | 16–18px                         | Comfortable line height around 1.5–1.65 and 55–75 characters per line.    |
| Working information  | 14–16px                         | Stable alignment and enough weight/contrast.                              |
| Metadata             | Usually 12–14px                 | Keep secondary facts readable; important facts must not become microtype. |

These are proposed scales to consolidate in tokens, with responsive testing. Use sentence case for most functional labels. Reserve widely spaced uppercase eyebrows for occasional short editorial labels; avoid 10px tracked text across every card. Use tabular numerals when comparing dates, quantities or cost.

## 12. Mark and layout rules

**Master asset:** `src/assets/indus-orbit-logo.png`. It is a gold/orange dimensional pixel `io` mark on a pale background. Do not claim that it contains an orbit ring or that it is a transparent vector master.

- Preserve its exact proportions, pixel geometry, built-in colour and shadow.
- Do not rotate, stretch, trace into a rounded `io`, add glow, recolour or apply the dreamy treatment to the mark.
- Use a proposed clear space of at least one quarter of the visible mark height, measured from its actual visible bounds. The current file has built-in canvas space; document optical bounds when a production asset pack is prepared.
- Keep the wordmark outside the raster, in the approved type treatment, where possible. Do not create a new wordmark from a merchandise mockup.
- Establish minimum sizes through actual rendering/export checks before calling them approved. Do not promise that a complex 512px raster will remain crisp at every favicon size.
- Place the master on a compatible calm surface. If a transparent or single-colour version is needed, prepare and review it as a separate master; do not use a screenshot or automatic background trick as a substitute.

For page layout, use generous but purposeful space, consistent content widths and a few surface types. Default to solid readable panels. A page should not need stacked glass, glow, gradient text, nested cards, large shadows and an illustration to explain one idea. Use a single strong editorial moment, then let the content do the work.

The current `/brand` instruction demanding 4.5:1 for every logo background misstates the text-contrast rule's logotype exception. A brand can set its own legibility quality rule, but it must not present that as a WCAG logo requirement. [W3C text contrast, logotype exception](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

## 13. Motion, sound and interaction

- Prefer 120–200ms for small state transitions and 200–300ms for a measured page/disclosure transition, subject to actual responsiveness. These are proposed defaults.
- Motion should show continuity, successful change or spatial relation.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; present the same information without movement.
- Avoid perpetual orbit/spin on task screens, flickering pixel stars, surprise scene movement on tab return and decorative loading that delays action.
- No ambient audio by default. Real voice/media should be intentional and controllable.
- Save/retry/offline language must match actual state. Do not imitate messaging security or delivery claims the system does not provide.

## 17. Accessibility and delivery standards

- Treat WCAG 2.2 AA as the web acceptance target. The documented palette does not certify every rendered state.
- Use a **44×44 CSS-pixel product target** for common touch controls where practical. WCAG 2.2 AA's minimum-size criterion is 24×24 CSS pixels or a documented exception; the larger product target is a deliberate usability standard, not a misquotation of AA. [W3C target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
- Test 320px layouts, 200% text zoom, keyboard paths, focus visibility, reduced motion and screen-reader names.
- Keep live text out of raster artwork. Decorative images should not create redundant screen-reader narration; meaningful art needs a concise description appropriate to its job.
- Preserve image dimensions, reserve space and provide responsive derivatives. Establish per-route transfer budgets from measured pages, including images and fonts.
- Avoid shipping full-resolution decorative downloads on every route. Load editorial/merchandise galleries only where relevant.
- Keep a rights/provenance ledger: asset, creator/source, consent/licence, original master, derivative, intended use, reviewer and date. Presence in Git is not proof of rights.
- Maintain actual screenshot acceptance for desktop/mobile and genuine product states. A mood board does not verify an implementation.
