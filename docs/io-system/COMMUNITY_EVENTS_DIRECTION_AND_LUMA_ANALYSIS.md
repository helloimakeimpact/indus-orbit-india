# Community and events direction — Luma analysis

Status: **Planned**, 2 October 2026. The owner explicitly directed Community toward a Luma-like, events-focused experience while preserving the core Indus Orbit identity. This changes the product priority and release backlog. It does not prove that the proposed registration, calendar or host features exist. The owner confirmed approved member hosts with review for new hosts, and public discovery with registration restricted to Community members. The owner selected native event workflows with free events first; Razorpay ticketing follows later. Current event foundations are **Partial**. Source-publication authorization persists; hosted migrations and launch approval remain separate.

## Executive decision

Make **events the invitation and communities the continuing relationship**. The Community product should help someone find a relevant gathering, understand its host and purpose, register confidently, attend, and continue building with the people they meet. The broad company remains **The General Intelligence Company of India**, with **Intelligence, built together.** as its approved line. Events express that mission through people gathering and doing useful work together.

This is an Indus Orbit implementation, using Luma as a product-quality reference. Preserve the original pixel mark, dawn/lotus/banyan artwork, indigo/parchment/saffron palette and Fraunces/Inter typography. Develop our own event templates, language, permission boundaries and host tools. I/O remains the intelligence/work capability within the wider company.

### Brand conclusion

My interpretation: Luma's appeal comes from a narrow, legible promise, an expressive event invitation and operational continuity from discovery through attendance. Its flexible covers and themes let hosts express a community's personality. These are useful principles for Indus Orbit; precise visual replication is neither specified nor needed.

**Our design thesis:** a warm invitation, a calm decision, a lasting relationship. This extends the six principles in `DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY_AND_UI_PLAN.md`: people first; evidence is beautiful; calm intensity; India in the details; continuity is respect; inclusive by construction.

Proposed Community description for review: **Meet people. Gather with purpose. Build what comes next.** This is supporting product copy, not a replacement for the approved company line. Event descriptions should answer: what happens, who it is for, who hosts it, when/where it happens, and what a guest should expect.

## Read-only hosted event authority review — 2 October

A connected metadata query confirms that hosted ACLs and RLS are not equivalent to the Community UI gate. No customer records were read and no hosted row/schema change was made. Evidence: `../release-evidence/community-2026-10-02/hosted-policy-metadata.json`.

- Hosted `events` grants SELECT to anon/authenticated and has an approved-event visibility policy. Define a public-safe field projection and separate sensitive location/join access before public discovery. Table grants alone do not prove a particular anonymous query succeeds; policy helper execution and actual negative personas still need testing.
- **Priority release blocker:** the legacy `Users can create events` INSERT check is only `auth.uid() = organizer_id`; it does not restrict the requested publication status or require an approved-host capability. Another chapter-lead INSERT policy does not narrow it, because permissive policies combine with OR. Harden publication/owner-change authority and test that ordinary members cannot self-approve, alter another host or bypass review. The new host model must be enforced server-side.
- RSVP INSERT checks owner identity, non-suspension and approved event, but does not check completed Community access. UPDATE checks owner identity only, without rechecking suspension/event eligibility. The member-required registration decision needs a canonical active-access check on every consequential transition, with chapter restrictions once specified.
- Guest rows have no anonymous SELECT policy even though table SELECT privilege exists. Hosted `event_rsvp_counts(uuid)` execution is denied to anon. Do not describe those guest lists/counts as publicly exposed from grants alone.
- Hosted contact/newsletter tables still have anonymous INSERT privileges and permissive insert policies. The new hCaptcha source cannot enforce hosted verification until its INSERT-revocation migration is delivered.
- Hosted authenticated SELECT grants on the form tables differ from fresh replay, which lacks the contact SELECT grant. Existing scoped admin read policies are present in both. Reconcile ACLs and prove the real admin read path without granting general browser read authority to make a test pass.

These are confirmed metadata findings, with runtime exploit/negative-persona checks still required. They are part of E0/E1 authorization work. Do not label the existing event submission/RSVP as a production-ready Luma-style registration system.

## Product structure

### Public entry

Proposed top-level navigation: **Events · Communities · Our work · About**, with a clear I/O entry and sign-in/account action. Exact navigation needs content review; preserve route access during rollout. The homepage should explain the company, then show a truthful invitation to upcoming gatherings and community participation. Never manufacture events, attendee numbers, host verification, urgency or photographs as live evidence.

Proposed routes (design only):

- `/events`: public-safe discovery, date/city/online/topic filters and clear empty state.
- `/events/$slug`: event invitation, organizer/community, timezone, registration state and safe location summary.
- `/communities` and `/communities/$slug`: public community story, calendars, hosts and follow preferences.
- `/app/events`: member schedule and discovery, preserving current access and continuity.
- A scoped host workspace: overview, guests, communications, event settings and check-in. Its exact route follows the host permission decision.

Do not add public reads to existing raw tables to implement these routes. Inventory currently exposed approved fields and define a minimal public projection; private addresses, join credentials and guest/contact data need separate authorization.

### Member home

Lead with the next relevant gathering and the member's schedule, followed communities and useful follow-up. Keep Messages, People, Learning and shared work reachable. Preserve drafts, selected filters and scroll on navigation and tab return. Relevance should be explainable from chosen city, community and topics; avoid an opaque engagement feed.

### Event page composition

Desktop: restrained cover/host/community column alongside title, schedule, location and a clear registration panel. Mobile: cover → title/host → time/place → registration → description/accessibility → community. A persistent mobile action is allowed only if it respects the keyboard, safe areas and screen-reader reading order.

Use real states: Register, Request a place, Join waitlist, Registration confirmed, Awaiting host approval, Waitlisted, Registration closed, Event cancelled, and Registration cancelled. Show price/currency/refund terms only for a working paid path. A saved or interested event must not display confirmation copy.

Use Indus Orbit's parchment for breathing room, indigo for the main structure, saffron for a primary decision, and monsoon green sparingly for community context. Keep editorial typography selective. Original art may frame the event invitation; the exact date, status, host and location remain readable text.

## Permissions and data design before implementation

1. **Separate entities:** event publication lifecycle, event visibility, registration status, payment status and attendance are independent. Preserve existing RSVPs during a reviewed migration; do not silently reinterpret all Going rows as approved registrations.
2. **Community/calendar home:** one managing community or organizer owns each event. Curating it into another community grants visibility through an explicit record; it does not transfer ownership or host authority.
3. **Registration identity:** the owner selected public discovery with Community membership required to register. Verify active membership server-side for registration and subsequent protected actions. A signed-in account alone is insufficient. Public visitors see a clear membership requirement and a route to the existing membership process; discovering or following an event never grants membership, private Space access or I/O budget. Confirm whether an event may additionally require its own chapter membership before implementing that narrower rule.
4. **Visibility:** legacy events retain their existing effective audience until reviewed. New public publication is explicit. Members-only content is enforced at database/API boundaries; precise venue/join access may require an approved registration even for a public invitation.
5. **Authority:** organizer, cohost, community/calendar manager, check-in staff and platform moderator have separately enumerated powers. Scope guest exports and message delivery explicitly; recheck suspended/removed access and step-up where appropriate.
6. **Capacity:** reservation/approval/cancellation uses atomic database operations under concurrency. Waitlist ordering and host overrides are explicit. Replayed requests are idempotent; no duplicate place, charge or notification.
7. **Time:** store canonical instants plus an IANA event timezone. Show the event timezone and useful local conversion; test multi-day, DST, midnight and online formats. The host chooses the authoritative zone.
8. **Tickets:** check-in tokens are opaque, short-scope and revocable. QR payloads contain no email or personal data. Restrict lookup, prevent repeated check-in and log corrections. Keep offline check-in out of the initial scope unless conflict resolution is designed and tested.
9. **Communication:** operational confirmations/changes and marketing/follow updates have separate purpose and preferences. Add deduplication, retries, dead-letter inspection, change/cancellation handling and accurate send evidence.
10. **Privacy and abuse:** host rules, cancellation/refund handling, abuse reporting, guest retention and event safety disclosures need named owners. hCaptcha protects public form submissions; it does not replace guest verification, rate controls or event moderation.
